# UI conventions

Small, pragmatic rules for the web UI (`src/MotiffLab.Web`) so that UI fixes stay consistent while music-core is the priority. The UI only displays what music-core returns: it never changes analysis or variation code, or the shapes of their results, and it never computes musical facts itself.

## Layout

- The workspace (`Pages/PlaybackLab.razor`, decision record 0012) follows the iPad mockups (PR #120): a header, the canvas on the left, the inspector on the right from about 64rem (below that it stacks under the canvas), and the transport fixed along the bottom.
- The header holds ‹ back to the project page (or to Projects for Für Elise), the piece and the session line (its intent and task), a folded "Open another piece", and what changes the whole view: **Score / Piano roll / Both** (a segmented group), **Focus**, Notes (the filter), Hear (Original, Variation, Both, and with a variation The piece with the variation and The original, then the variation; see Compare) and Score (As written, Generated from the notes, With the variation), each a labelled select. The Score select is disabled while only the roll shows.
- The canvas is the phrase strip, then the score (ink on paper, scrolling within its panel), then the piano roll with its keyboard. With both views a **divider** sits between them: drag it, or focus it and use the arrow keys, Home and End; it is a `role="separator"` with `aria-valuenow` in percent of the canvas. On a wide screen, and in Focus mode at any width, the canvas fills the window between the header and the transport (`Scripts/layout/workspace-layout.ts` marks it `data-fill` and measures the chrome into `--ml-chrome`); the score takes `--ml-split` of it and the roll the rest. The roll never collapses: given less room than its notes need it scrolls within itself. The view and the divider are device preferences (`Projects/Preferences.cs`, keys `ml-view` and `ml-split`), never project data.
- **Focus** (`PlaybackLab.Layout.cs`) hides the header, the inspector, the phrase tools and the footer and shows the Focus bar: Back to exploring, the selection text, previous and next phrase, Loop, the compare switch while a variation exists, and Score / Piano roll / Both. It asks for full screen where the browser allows and works the same where it does not. Controls on the Focus bar and the transport are at least 44px. Focus is not kept across a reload.
- The inspector stays in view as the canvas scrolls. On top it says what is selected, with Loop and Clear, and while a variation exists the compare switch (see Compare); under that four tabs: **Practise** (play, loop and slow down the phrase), **Understand** (the facts, with their places and the experiments resting on them, and a hand-off to Explore on the same passage), **Explore** (the Explore card, Compare and Kept for this phrase) and **Tools** (the scan check, Keep preset and chips, which notes a step changes, the operations, the steps, What changed, hear and export; editing variation notes and correcting imported notes; a second file and the tracks). The three ways of working share the selection, the tempo and the loop. Put new selection tools in the tab they belong to, not under the canvas.
- The tabs read one **workspace state** (`Workspace/WorkspaceState.cs`, decision record 0013 section 6): the page builds a snapshot for each render (the selection with its phrase context, playback, the variation and its trial, the facts and the chosen one, the ideas) and cascades it; a tab, the compare switch and the phrase strip read it through `[CascadingParameter] WorkspaceState State` and ask for a change through their callbacks. Put state a tab needs into the snapshot, never into a new parameter or into the component, so switching tabs changes nothing.
- The transport: a round lime play button, Stop, the position slider and text, the state, tempo, metronome and count-in. In Focus mode on a narrow screen the state, metronome and count-in are hidden.
- Tokens are on `:root` in `wwwroot/css/app.css`: charcoal ground, `--ml-panel` and `--ml-surface` for raised panels, lime for what is on, `--ml-inferred` (blue) and `--ml-uncertain` (amber) for certainty, and a monospace stack for positions and counts. Buttons are rounded (0.6rem) with a `--ml-border` outline; on or primary ones are lime.
- The address names the open project and session (`playback?project=p_…&session=s_…`), so a reload or a link opens it again from the device; `&idea=o_…` opens an idea on arrival. Für Elise has no project, so its address has none.
- There are no bar number boxes: selection is made on the roll or in the score (a click on a bar away from its notes; Shift-click extends), or, with the roll focused, the keyboard.

## Projects, sessions and ideas

- Projects (`Pages/Home.razor`) opens with **Start with a piece**: four tiles, at least 44px and two to a row on a tablet, four from 64rem: Open a file (`import`), Scan printed music (`import/scan`), Notes from a recording (`import/recording`), each its own screen whose picker takes only what that way reads, and Try Für Elise (`playback?start=fur-elise`, which opens the example as soon as the player is ready). The scan and recording tiles say in amber when they need a server or a sign-in first, from the same states the import screen explains. Under them, Import a project file.
- Then the pieces as cards, each a link to the project page (`Pages/ProjectPage.razor`, `/project/{id}`), with Open, Export and Delete, and those saved online but not here; with no pieces yet, a short How it works instead.
- A link to a place on another screen (`settings#account`) lands there: the layout scrolls to the fragment after each new page (`Scripts/layout/fragment.ts`).
- The project page shows the piece (bars, sessions, ideas), **What do you want to do?** with the four intents (Practise, Understand, Explore, Just open the piece) and an optional task in the player's words, **Recent sessions** (Continue, End session) and **Saved ideas** (Open). Starting or continuing a session opens the workspace on the tab the intent asks for and on the passage the session was last on.
- Everything kept in a session is marked with it (`Observation.SessionId`), shown as an idea on the project page; ending a session keeps its ideas with the piece. The UI never builds a session or an observation itself: it asks the project (`StartSession`, `ChangeSession`, `Keep`).
- Saving online (0014) is reached only through `ProjectStore`: `SaveAsync` and `DeleteAsync` repeat the change online when the player turned it on, and `SaveHereAsync` keeps a project fetched from online on the device only. Pages never call the API themselves; anything about accounts shows only when `CloudAccount.IsAvailable`, and an online failure is said on Settings, never in the way of the piece.
- Account and Settings follow familiar apps: Projects has the language (RU | EN) and the account at the top right (`Account/AccountButton.razor`): Sign in while signed out, the player's initial once signed in, which opens the player's own page (`/settings`, titled Your account). The footer has only Privacy and Terms of use. The account page holds signing in (Sign in and Create an account together) or, signed in, who it is, saving online and erasing it. Scanning and transcription always use Motiff Lab's server where the build has one, with nothing to set; only a build without it (running the app locally) shows a Settings gear and the address of one's own scan server. The language is set in the header only, and analytics consent sits on the privacy notice next to what each event holds (`Analytics/AnalyticsConsent.razor`). Elsewhere, what needs an account says so in one line with a Sign in link (`Account/SignInPrompt.razor`), never a block of buttons; Create an account opens External ID's sign-up form (`prompt=create`), and the sign-in pages follow the app's language (`ui_locales`, `mkt`). Switches and choices are at least 44px.

## Localization

- Interface strings live in `wwwroot/i18n/en.json` and `ru.json`, read through `UiText` (`Text["key"]`, `Text.Format("key", args)`); a missing key falls back to English, then to the key. Add every new user-facing string to both files. The language is chosen on Settings and kept as a device preference; `Text.Changed` tells pages to render again.
- Do not translate what music-core returns (fact text, notes, bar labels); that is a music-core concern.

## Phrases

- The piece is shown as phrases (#18, `Phrases/PhraseStrip.razor`): a strip above the roll with one chip per phrase from `PhraseMap`, its number, its bars and how its start was found (slur, rest, repeat sign, comes back, yours, or start). Certainty shows by line as well as colour: solid for marks in the file and the player's own boundaries, dashed blue for inferred ones. The chip's accessible name and hover text say the whole reason and the confidence.
- Picking a chip selects the phrase as the working context; Practise, Explore and the facts read it. A phrase longer than a selection holds (8 bars) is selected up to 8 bars, and the selection text says so. The selection text names the phrase ("Selected phrase 2, bars 4–8").
- **Make the selection a phrase** turns any other selection into a phrase (moving or splitting boundaries; an edge less than a beat from a boundary keeps that boundary), **Join with the next phrase** takes away the boundary after the selected phrase, and **Back to the suggested phrases** forgets the player's boundaries. Edits are saved with the project; the UI never computes a boundary itself.
- A phrase with kept observations shows how many on its chip ("2 kept"), and its accessible name says so (see Observations).
- Where the file gives little evidence, the strip says so and points to selecting bars; no boundary is invented.
- The roll marks each phrase start with a flag on the bar numbers and a faint line, and the score with a labelled line through the system (`setPhrases` in the roll and score scripts); both dashed and blue where inferred. The selected phrase's label in the score is reversed (white on ink, or on blue when inferred), and the first phrase is marked only while it is selected, so the score shows where the phrase sits in the piece.
- **‹** and **›** at either end of the chips select the previous and the next phrase (#126), from the phrase the selection stands for or mostly lies in; with nothing selected, **›** selects the first. Page Up and Page Down do the same anywhere on the page (`Scripts/phrases/phrase-keys.ts`), except in a field or with a modifier; Bluetooth page-turner pedals send these keys. Stepping keeps the tab, the tempo, the loop and what is heard.
- **Add the previous phrase** and **Add the next phrase** (Hear the transition) run the selection on into its neighbour (`PhraseMap.ExtendInto`); the phrases themselves stay as they are. A selection holds at most 8 bars, so the moving edge stops there and the selection text says so; the button is disabled when nothing more can be added. A selection from one phrase start to a later phrase's end reads "Selected phrases 1–2, bars …".

## Selection on the piano roll

- **Drag anywhere** on the roll (the bar numbers or the notes area) to select a range. music-core snaps it to beats (`MotifSelector.Drag`); the roll only reports where the drag started and ended.
- **Drag an edge** of the selection (grips on the bar numbers, an ew-resize cursor anywhere on the edge line) to move just that edge; the other edge stays. A reshape in place is made again on the new range.
- **Click a bar number** to select that bar. **Shift-click a bar number** to extend the selection to that bar, in whole bars.
- **By keyboard**, with the roll focused: **]** and **[** select the bar after or before the selection (the first bar when nothing is selected), and with **Shift** they extend the selection by that bar, as a click or Shift-click on its number does (WCAG 2.1.1). Space loops the selection.
- **Click a note** to hear it and select it; Shift, Ctrl or Cmd adds to the note selection. A press on a note that moves becomes a range drag.
- **Clear** with Escape, a click on an empty place in the roll, or a click on the page outside the roll. Clicks on controls (buttons, inputs, links, labels) never clear. Panels that show or act on the selection carry `data-keeps-selection`, so reading or using them does not clear it: today the inspector and the score. Mark any new panel of that kind the same way.
- **Space** plays and pauses from anywhere on the page: with notes selected it loops them and plays (later presses pause and resume the loop), with nothing selected it plays the piece. It acts on buttons and sliders too (Enter still presses a button), but not in text fields, lists, checkboxes or radios, where Space types or ticks.
- The 8-bar limit and its message come from music-core (`MotifSelection.MaxBars`).

## The selection in the score, and the range that plays

- The score shows the same selection as the roll: notes that start outside the range that plays are drawn faint, as on the roll, and the first note in the range is scrolled into view. Both views take the range from the page (`ShownRange`), so they never disagree with each other or with what loops.
- The range that plays is the selection's range while the original is heard. While the variation is heard it runs from the selection's start to the end of the variation's last note, rounded up to the beat: a variation that runs longer (half-time) extends it, one that runs shorter (double-time) shortens it. Heard together with the original (Both), it never ends before the selection. It never runs past the end of the piece.
- The roll marks the range that plays with a white line over the bar numbers; where it differs from the selection it adds a dashed end line, and a light band where it runs past the selection. The selection itself (lime band and edges) stays as the player made it.
- Applying, changing or removing a variation never changes the selection. While the selection loops, the loop follows what is heard and the variation's length; changing Hear while it loops keeps looping.

## Pencil marks on the score

- The player writes on the score by hand (decision record 0015, `Score/ScoreView.razor`, `Scripts/score/pencil-layer.ts`). A pen (the Apple Pencil) writes at any time; a finger never writes and still scrolls; a pen tap picks a note or a bar as a click does. The **✎ Pencil** button above the score turns the tools on (writing with the pen turns them on too): pen in black, red or blue, Highlighter, Eraser, Undo and Clear marks. With them on, the mouse writes as well. Controls are at least 44px.
- Marks are kept against the bar they start in and drawn again after every layout; the page passes the piece's key (`PencilMarks.KeyFor`) for its own score, as written or generated, and none for the score with a variation in it. Marks whose bar is not in the score shown are kept and counted next to the tools.
- Marks are the player's writing, never music: no analysis, fact or variation reads them. They stay on the device and go with their project when it is deleted.

## Practise

- The Practise tab (`Practise/PractisePanel.razor`, #50) is the "I'm just practising" way of working: **Play from the start** (of the phrase; Pause while playing), **Loop the phrase**, **Previous phrase** and **Next phrase**, the tempo with − and + in steps of 5% and presets 50%, 75% and 100%, and what to hear while the phrase loops. Controls are large enough to reach with the iPad on the music stand. Nothing asks the player to do a task and nothing scores them.
- Hear offers **Right hand** and **Left hand** when the phrase has notes in both (as the file marks them or as inferred, and the panel says when some are inferred), and **Melody alone** and **Accompaniment alone** when it has a melody over an accompaniment. Choosing one loops the phrase; the selection text says what loops.
- **Play on to the end** plays from the start of the phrase to the end of the piece and **Play the whole piece** from its start, with every note; the loop stops and the selection stays, so the roll and the score keep showing where the phrase is. **Back to the phrase** then pauses, goes back to the start of the phrase and loops it again if it looped before. The selection text says what plays.
- Practise only drives the page's playback state, which every tab shares: switching to Understand or Explore and back keeps the phrase, the tempo, the loop and the hand. Moving to another phrase keeps them too, unless the new phrase has no notes for that hand or layer.

## Hearing notes by hand

- Clicking a note, a key on the keyboard under the roll, or a pitch name in the roll's left gutter plays that key at once, also while the piece plays; dragging across keys plays each in turn. These notes go to the engine's `live` source: transport, source and track changes never cut them, and they never touch the piece or its playback.

## Colour and the Notes filter

- Colour by Track, Voice or Hand. A grouping with only one group in the piece (one track; no notated voices; no hands) is disabled with its reason next to the control, never left as a control that does nothing. The roll starts with the first grouping that tells notes apart: tracks, else hands, else voices.
- The Notes filter offers what music-core can pick out: hands, tracks, and notated voices of a track (`SelectionFilter`). It limits what plays (the original source), what is drawn at full strength and lights up on the roll, and what a selection keeps. Changing it keeps the selection's range and makes a reshape in place again.
- Melody, bass and harmony are listed but disabled until music-core can find them (voice and chord analysis, S1-A3). Add them as filters only when music-core provides them.

## Explore

- The Explore card (`Explore/ExploreCard.razor`, decision record 0010) shows only what `ExperimentCatalogue` offers for the selection: with nothing tried, each experiment's title and question; picking one tries its first alternative and plays it at once. Never build an experiment in the UI.
- An experiment on the card, as in the mockup: the title, what changes, the question, a chip with the passage ("Selected phrase · bar 1", or "The motif's return · bar 5"), its alternatives as one-click buttons, what it keeps, What changed, **Original** and **Variation** (the compare switch's, see Compare), the listening focus, then **Keep this variation**, **Try another** (the next alternative, then the next experiment) and **Leave it**. Other ideas for the phrase are listed under the card.
- Trying an experiment sets the Keep chips to its preset and replaces the variation. It is saved with the project only once kept. **Leave it** puts back the selection, Keep choices and steps the player had; selecting other bars before keeping drops the experiment and puts back the Keep choices and steps. Changing Keep or adding a step builds on the experiment instead.
- An experiment on the motif's return moves the selection there and says so; **Hear the motif, then the answer** loops from the motif to the end of the return with the piece around them (the Hear choice The piece with the variation). The roll marks that range as the range that plays.
- With a melody over an accompaniment, **Everything**, **Melody alone** and **Accompaniment alone** choose what plays while the passage loops. The layers come from music-core's texture reading (`OperationScopes`), not from the hands, and the selection text says which one loops.
- Under the listening focus, **Rests on:** lists the facts the experiment cites (`Experiment.FactIds`), with their text from the analysis of the selection it was offered for. Each is a button that opens Understand with that fact chosen and highlighted.
- **Surprise me** asks music-core for an experiment not tried yet since the piece was opened; **I have a question** lists the questions, each disabled with its reason when nothing in the selection answers it.

## Compare

Return to the original and compare (#43, step 6 of `docs/08-central-product-model.md`), so the player notices whether their understanding changed.

- **The compare switch** (`Compare/CompareSwitch.razor`) sits in the inspector head on every tab while a variation exists: **Original | Variation** (aria-pressed) and **One after the other**. Original hears the original phrase and shows the original score (as written when the file has one and no note was corrected, else generated); the variation and its steps stay. Variation hears it and shows With the variation. A running loop keeps looping, at the same place, with the new choice; otherwise the passage loops from its start. The Explore card's Original and Variation do the same.
- **One after the other** loops the original phrase, then the variation (over the range it takes), then again; the Hear select offers it too as The original, then the variation, and any other Hear choice, Original, Variation or the Looping button ends it. The engine switches on the exact frame (an alternating loop, decision record 0007) and reports the pass; the selection text says "Now the original" or "Now the variation" (shown, not announced at every pass), the sounding button is outlined (aria-current), the roll draws the one sounding at full strength with its range, and the score shows its version. The score is engraved again at each pass, so on a long piece it can follow the sound a moment late; the sound never waits for it. While the two take turns the score keeps the taller of their heights (`HoldHeight`), so a tablet's inspector stacked under it does not jump at every pass.
- **Compare** (`Compare/ComparePanel.razor`) is under the Explore card and next to What changed in the Selection tab: What changed counts the diff (`Variation.Changes`, `Added`, `Removed`: pitch, start and length of how many of the original's notes) and names the properties the checks found changed; What was kept lists the Keep checks (`Variation.Checks`) as kept, no longer kept and not checked, with the property names, then the properties not asked for that stayed the same (`Variation.Properties`); then the rule flags (`Variation.Flags`). Only what the diff and the checks say; nothing is computed again in the UI.

## Observations

Recording observations (#78, `docs/08-central-product-model.md`): the player keeps what they found about a phrase and comes back to it. Each is an `Observation` from music-core (`MotiffLab.MusicCore.Projects`); the UI only lists, opens and changes them through `Project` (`Keep`, `RenameObservation`, `ChangeObservationNote`, `DeleteObservation`) and never edits one itself. The page code is `Pages/PlaybackLab.Observations.cs`; the components are in `Observations/`.

- **Keep this variation** on the Explore card keeps the phrase the experiment was offered for, the experiment and alternative, the variation as its steps (`KeptVariation`), and a name ("Give the melody more space: Short"). The card then offers **Add a note in your own words (optional)**, saved when the field loses focus or with **Save note**.
- **The note is the player's text.** It is stored as written (trimmed) and shown as written, after "Your note:"; nothing reads, scores, summarises or sends it. It never goes into analytics, and no analytics event says whether there is one.
- **Kept for phrase N** (`Observations/ObservationList.razor`) sits in the Explore tab under the card and Compare. It lists the observations about the phrase the selection is (or lies mostly in), by overlap (`Observation.IsAbout`), in the order they were kept: the name, the bars, where the variation came from (an experiment, your steps, a note), and the note. The one heard now has a lime edge and says "You hear this", so it differs by more than colour; its Open is disabled.
- **Open** selects the variation's passage with its filter, puts back its Keep choices and its steps (`Observation.Restore`, with the key it ran with), and plays it at once, as trying an experiment does; the selection text says "Opened “name”." From there Original, Variation, One after the other and Compare work as usual. If the notes no longer fit (a correction removed one), the list says why and nothing changes.
- **Rename** edits the name in place (Enter saves, Escape cancels); **Add a note** or **Edit note** opens the note field; **Export MIDI** hands over the kept variation through the storage script, named by `Observation.MidiFileName`; **Delete** asks first (**Delete for good** or **Keep**), since it cannot be undone.
- **Keep the variation you hear** keeps a variation made with the player's own steps (named by its steps), when it is not kept already; an experiment being tried is kept from its card. **Write a note about this phrase** keeps a note alone about the selection.
- **Saved, or not, said plainly.** With a project, every change is saved with it at once on the device (project file version 6). Für Elise has no project (a file opened from the workspace's Open file is saved as a project, as an imported one is): the card says "Kept until you leave the page; this piece is not saved as a project." and the list says what is kept goes when the page is left. Nothing is kept for another piece: loading one starts with its own project, or none.

## Variations

- Each operation's parameters are one-click buttons that apply and play at once (S1-E3); Keep properties are toggle chips that turn lime when kept and dashed when the step broke them.
- The Selection tab lists the operations of the catalogue this version has (V1, V13, V2, V4, V9, V10), each a folding group named by its id; V1 is open at first. An open group says why the Keep choices rule it out, and each button says why it cannot run, from music-core. **Change** limits the next step to the melody, the accompaniment or the bass for the operations that can be limited (V1, V13); the others change every selected note and say so.
- The roll draws the variation as white outlines where its notes land, with a line from each note it moved; the motif's own notes go faint behind it. The score can show the piece with the variation in place ("With the variation"), generated from the notes and so marked inferred; making the first variation switches the score to it.
- A variation is a chain of steps (`VariationChain`): a parameter button proposes a step on top of the accepted ones; **Accept step**, **Reject step**, **Undo** and **Redo** sit in one row with the breadcrumbs ("Original › Half-time › trying Later by 1 eighth"; a limited step reads "Detached (the melody)"). Selection and Keep changes replay the chain; if a step no longer runs, the variation is cleared. Rule flags (`Variation.Flags`) are listed under the explanation, never hidden.
- Draw variations from `Variation.Changes` (before and after per note), never by recomputing the operation in the UI.
- **Export motif MIDI** and **Export variation MIDI** hand over the file `MidiExporter` writes (S1-X1), named by `MidiExporter.FileName` after the project, the bars and the steps. The download goes through the storage script; nothing leaves the device.

## Correcting imported notes

- **Correct notes** (S1-I7) is a collapsible table of the selected notes under the variation controls: each row nudges pitch by a semitone, onset and length by a 16th, and velocity by 10, or removes the note. Edited rows are marked "Edited by you" as well as by the lime edge.
- Every correction goes through `NoteEditLog`; the UI never changes a note itself. **Undo correction** drops the last edit and **Revert** drops every edit of one note, so the imported value comes back exactly. Removed notes are listed with a Revert button each.
- The project saves the log, and opening a project applies it. After the first correction the score switches from the file as written to the score generated from the notes, since the file does not know the corrections.

## Editing variation notes

- **Edit notes by hand** (S1-E7) is a collapsible table inside the variation controls, with the same nudges as Correct notes plus **Add after** (a note of the same pitch and length straight after) and **Remove**. It lists the notes as heard; with no steps yet it lists the selected notes, and the first edit starts a hand-made variation.
- Every edit goes through `VariationChain.Edit` as one step: a reshape being tried is accepted first, Undo and Redo take edits back like any other step, and the explanation shows "This edit" before "From the original". Keep chips are checked again after each edit and are never a reason to block it (S1-E9). A reason why an edit cannot apply is shown under the table.

## Understand

- The Understand tab (#25) shows the facts by `Understand/UnderstandPanel.razor`, one collapsible section per `FactLens`, in the enum's order. A new fact, or a new lens added to `FactLens`, appears without UI changes. Pass every analysis's facts to the panel in one list. The tab label stays "Understand" without a count, so the five tabs fit on one line.
- Each section header gives the lens, the number of facts and how many are detected, inferred or uncertain, so a closed section still shows how sure it is. Closed sections stay closed as the selection changes.
- Every fact keeps its certainty badge and its basis. The badge differs by line as well as colour: solid grey for detected, dashed blue for inferred, dotted amber for uncertain. The basis is the hover text and also opens under the fact with its Basis button, so it can be read on touch. Never drop either.
- **Places.** A fact with places (`MotifFact.Places`, from music-core) is a toggle button ("Show 2 places", `aria-pressed`). Choosing it highlights all its places in the score and on the roll, says so in a box at the top of the tab with **Clear highlight**, and lists its places as chips by their labels. A chip highlights only its place and moves playback to its start; pressed again, all places come back. Choosing the fact again, Clear highlight, or any change of the selection clears the highlight. The UI never works out a place; facts without places (the key, a steady tempo) are plain text.
- **In the score** (`setHighlight` in `Scripts/score/score-view.ts`), a place's notes are blue (`--ml-score-highlight`, the information blue on paper, 4.8 : 1 on white), at full strength even outside the range that plays, with a solid box round each notehead, so they differ from the notes being heard (dark lime, colour only) and the notes outside the range (faint) by more than colour. Engraved ids are Verovio's, so notes are matched by when they sound (`highlightedIds` in `score-timeline.ts`): an engraved note stands for a place's note when it has the same MIDI key and starts while that note sounds (tied pieces included), or, in a generated score, when it carries the note's own id. A place with no notes of its own (a phrase, a silence) takes every engraved note and rest that starts in it. A score with the variation in place highlights only the notes the variation left as they were.
- **On the roll** (`setHighlight` in `Scripts/roll/piano-roll.ts`), each place gets a faint blue band and a solid blue underline along the bottom, and its notes a solid blue box with a dark edge, drawn at full strength; the legend explains it. The selection stays lime and the variation white, and inferred marks stay dashed, so the highlight differs by line as well as colour. Both views draw it again after a relayout or a new score.
- **Links.** Under a fact, **Try: <title>** lists the offered experiments resting on it (`ExperimentCatalogue.RestingOn` over the page's offers); it opens Explore and tries the first alternative (analytics entry "fact"). While an experiment offered for another passage runs (the motif's return), the facts shown are that passage's and the offers rest on other facts, so no Try links show. The Explore card links back through **Rests on:**; a fact chosen from there that belongs to the passage the experiment was offered for is shown in the highlight box with its chips, and says so.
- Everything here is template text from music-core and works with AI off.

## General

- Group related controls into one row (`.row`) or panel; prefer a collapsible section with a summary over more lines of text.
- Collapsible sections use a `<button aria-expanded>` header with the state held in the component, not `<details>`, so Blazor re-renders keep the state.
- Colours come from the brand tokens in `wwwroot/css/app.css` (`docs/02-brand-identity.md`); anything inferred is marked as such, by more than colour.
