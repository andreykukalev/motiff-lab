# 0016 · Bars and a tempo for notes heard in a recording

Status: accepted, 2026-10-30; revised 2026-10-31 (items 6 to 12). Andrei: Transkun hears the notes almost perfectly and its MIDI is clean, but the score still looks frightening; the problem is now the notation, not the recognition. Then, comparing a freely played recording of Paterlini's "Winter Stories" with the printed score: the app wrote no key, the pedal made long notes and extra voices, the grid was too fine, the downbeat was half a bar off, and the rubato defeated the single tempo. "The main thing is to pass on the motif": quantize, as post-processing. And two modes: one to read, one as close to the playing as notation goes. Then: the playback sounds detached, so hold the notes with the pedal; mark Ped., legato and crescendos on the notes. And: pieces with triplets.

## Context

The transcription engine (0014, Transkun 2.0.1) writes what it hears as MIDI at 120 BPM in 4/4, with each note at the time it was played. The score view (0008) writes those notes in the file's bars on a 32nd grid. The bar lines therefore fall at arbitrary places in the music, every onset is a little off any beat, and a pianist's slightly early key releases turn into short notes followed by short rests. On Für Elise heard from a recording, the notes were right and the page was unreadable.

A prototype on that recording (904 notes, 130 s) showed that three steps make the generated score read almost like the printed one: find the beat, put bar lines where the bass falls, and write a note held to the next one when the gap is shorter than an eighth.

## Decision

1. **The bars and tempo are an inference about the notes, not a change to them.** `BarGrid` (music-core, `Import/Transcription`) finds a 16th note in microseconds and a grid origin that leave the onsets closest to a steady beat, relative to the beat's length: a coarse search over the first 16 seconds (16ths of 80 to 500 ms), a finer one over the first 32, then a least-squares line over ever longer stretches up to the whole piece, all in integer arithmetic. When the shortest notes found read as a quarter slower than 60 a minute, they are taken as eighths. Onsets that sit on average more than a sixth of a step from the grid follow no steady beat, and no bars are offered.
2. **Bar lines go where the bass falls.** Onsets whose lowest note is below middle C and the lowest within a dotted eighth either way are likely downbeats. For each bar length offered (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 3/8, 6/8) the downbeat's place is judged over four bars around each bar line; when it moves for two windows running (a repeat, a held beat), that bar is cut short and the bars start afresh. The bars that hold the most likely downbeats, better than chance and less a cost per short bar, are chosen; within a tenth of the best, 4/4 is preferred, then the shorter bar (3/8 over 6/8 or 3/4, which the bass cannot tell apart), and 6/8 over 3/4 when the bass falls on the half bar more than on the second and third quarters. A first downbeat after the first note makes a pickup.
3. **Applying the grid moves no note.** `BarGrid.Apply` re-expresses every note's onset and end, through `QuarterFraction` and `CanonicalTime`, against the found beat, with one tempo of four 16ths a quarter, so playback sounds as the recording did. Ids, pitches, velocities, hands and attributions are kept. The meter is a `MeterMap` with the chosen signature, the pickup, and a change of the same signature at each place the bars start afresh. The import report drops the engine's 120 BPM and 4/4 notices and adds an Assumed notice (`transcription.bars-inferred`) that names the signature, the tempo, the pickup and the short bars. The tempo fact (S1-A5) says the tempo was worked out from the notes.
4. **The player sees and controls it.** After transcription the import screen shows the bars and tempo found, with the time signature to choose, twice or half the tempo, and a switch back to the engine's bars. The choice is stored as the grid itself in the project's `TranscriptionOrigin` (project file version 10: origin tick, 16th in microseconds, signature, bar starts; version 11 adds the beats), not worked out again on open, so a later change to the search never moves a saved project's notes or its corrections. The engine's MIDI stays the project's source.
5. **Held notes in the score.** For notes heard in a recording (MIDI whose notes are all detected), the score view writes a note that ends less than an eighth before the next onset on its staff up to that onset (`NotationGenerator`'s `holdShortGaps`). The notes keep their lengths; the score note under the view counts the notes shown held.

6. **The tempo follows the playing.** The grid stores a list of beats, each the time in the recording at which a beat falls, and how many 16ths a beat is written as (`StepsPerBeat`, 1 to 16); after the last beat, beats continue at the typical length. When the bass keeps a steady beat (at least 16 bass notes, the lowest of their neighbours and below middle C, whose gaps are mostly about the typical gap between 0.3 and 2 s, half of it or twice it), the beats follow the bass: each is expected one beat after the last, moves to the nearest bass note within a quarter of a beat, failing that to any onset within an eighth, and the expected length follows the last few beats within 0.6 to 1.6 of the first. A bass beat is written as the fewest of 2, 4, 8 or 16 16ths that leave a 16th no longer than 300 ms, or half as many again when triplets of them leave the onsets far closer. Otherwise the 16th found as in item 1 starts a beat of an eighth, tracked the same way from every onset; onsets that sit on average more than an eighth of a step from the bending beat follow no beat. `Apply` writes a tempo change on every beat, so playback still sounds as recorded. Twice or half the tempo writes the same beats in twice or half as many 16ths. Project file version 11 adds the beats and the 16ths per beat; bars from version 10 keep one tempo. The tempo fact sums such a tempo up (its typical value and range) instead of listing every change.
7. **The key from the notes.** A transcription has no key signature, so the score is written in the major or minor key whose Krumhansl–Kessler profile best fits how long each pitch class sounds, with notes below middle C counting twice, since the bass carries the roots (`Transcribed.KeyOf`). It is a key for the score only: analysis still treats the piece as having none, and the score note says it was worked out.
8. **Two ways to write the notes.** The score view offers "For reading" (the default) and "As played" for notes heard in a recording; the sound is the recording either way. For reading (`NotationGenerator`'s `asHeard`, replacing `holdShortGaps`): onsets within a 64th are one chord; onsets go to the nearest 16th, a 32nd where 16ths would merge two; a single note shorter than a 32nd just before the next is a grace note; a chord is written with the length of its longest note, cut at the next onset on its staff (the pedal) and held to it when the gap is shorter than a quarter, so each hand reads as one line. As played (`playedTempos`): the 32nd grid of 0008 with the real lengths, plus marks inferred from the playing: dynamics from the median velocity of the melody (at or above middle C) over each bar and its neighbours, written when the level changes and holds for the next bar; accents on notes at least 16 and a quarter louder than that median in their register; and "rit.", "accel." and "a tempo" where the bars, three at a time, grow a seventh longer or shorter than the piece's usual bar and come back within a fourteenth. Voices leave empty bars out, and the top voice rests for a whole bar only when no voice plays.
9. **Bar lines a beat earlier or later.** The bass can mistake beat 3 for beat 1 or miss a downbeat; the import screen moves every bar line by a beat of the signature either way (`BarGrid.Shifted`).
10. **The pedal holds the sound.** Transkun hears the sustain pedal and writes it as controller 64. The MIDI importer now keeps it (`MidiImport.SustainPedal`: each press from a value of 64 or more to the next below 64, held to the end of the track when never let go) instead of only counting it, and `BarGrid.Apply` moves it with the notes. Playback holds a note whose key is let go while the pedal is down until the pedal comes up, or until the same key on the same track is struck again; a pedal lifted and pressed again at once still damps it. Only the sound changes: the notes keep their lengths, so the score, analysis and Keep checks are unchanged, and a variation played on the piece's timeline is held by the same pedal. Any MIDI file with the pedal plays this way, not only a transcription.
11. **More marks as played.** "As played" also writes, all inferred: "Ped." and "*" under the bottom staff where the pedal was held; hairpins where the melody's velocity, averaged over five onsets, rises or falls by at least 12 over at least four onsets and a half note (a turn counts once it has come back 12 from its highest or lowest), written as "cresc." or "dim." when longer than a bar; and slurs over the top staff's melody (the highest note at each onset) where at least three notes follow each other within a half note with the key held to within a 64th of the next, two bars at most, ending at a bar line. "For reading" stays without them. On "Winter Stories" (39 bars): 61 pedal marks, 22 crescendos and diminuendos, 34 slurs.
12. **Triplets.** Per staff and beat of a simple meter (not 6/8, 9/8 or 12/8, which already divide by three), `NotationGenerator` writes the beat as a tuplet when its onsets, taken from a 12th of a beat early, lie on thirds of the beat (three eighths in a quarter, `num="3" numbase="2"`) or sixths (triplet 16ths, `num="6" numbase="4"`): at least one onset off the 16ths, half the total distance from that grid that they have from the 16ths, none further than a 12th of the beat from a third or a 24th from a sixth, and no triplet value shorter than a 32nd (so not in 3/8 for sixths). A beat with only two onsets (a quarter and an eighth) counts only next to a beat with three or more, so that a dotted rhythm played a little short stays dotted. Onsets and ends in the beat go on its grid, in both ways of writing; a voice writes the tuplet only where it starts or ends a note between the 16ths, so triplets against eighths stay in the hand that plays them. Quarter-note triplets over two beats are not found yet. It applies to every generated score, so a MIDI file with exact triplets is written with them too; the score note counts them. On Für Elise as heard, 15 beats of the closing runs; on "Winter Stories", 32 beats, mostly a right hand of six notes to the beat.

## Consequences

- The first transcription of a piece opens in readable bars without the player doing anything, and every inference is named where it is shown.
- A tempo that changes a lot is followed when the bass keeps the beat; without a steady bass the beat bends from the onsets alone and may slip by a step, which cuts a bar short. Restore rhythm (S1-R1–R7) can build on the beats.
- Triplets and sextuplets within a beat are written as tuplets (item 12); quarter-note triplets over two beats still land on the nearest 16ths.
- On "Winter Stories" the bars and tempo now read as in print (4/4, a quarter at about 57 against the printed 65, F minor), with the downbeat a beat late where the recording lost the first bass note; the shift buttons correct it.
- Projects saved by this version open in earlier versions only after updating (version 11), as with every format change.
