# 0018 · The redesign: the score as the whole screen, blue pencil on cream paper

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-01 · **Scope:** the whole user interface of the web app (`src/MotiffLab.Web`) and of the native iPad app; the project file (version 12, in its own records); the design kit in `design/kit`. Andrei wrote the two documents this record rests on, *Motiff Lab UX Concept* and *Motiff Lab Visual Design System* (both 2026-10-31), delivered the design kit (`tokens.css`, `ds.css`, `icons.js`, `score.js`, the Verovio options, eleven screens and four boards) and answered the open questions on 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01. The preparation list, with what must land in the engine and the data model before the first screen changes, is the Claude Doc [Что сделать до редизайна](https://claude.ai/artifact/TVHGtpHFAG5uksrVznyK7v); this record is item 16 of it.

## Context

The current build (main after PR #177) does a lot: three import routes with per-bar confidence reports, detected phrases with manual adjustment, loop and tempo practice, hand and melody isolation, stylus ink, facts with evidence, experiments that produce variations, a build-your-own-variation tool, sessions and saved ideas, A4 pages with a PDF, Russian for the interface and the musical layer. What gets in the way is how it is laid out, and the UX concept names it:

1. Three overlapping mode systems (the view tabs, Listen, the score variant) sit in a header that wraps to two lines even in landscape, and Focus mode repeats them.
2. The phrase is the unit of work ([[08-central-product-model]], [[0012]]) but does not live on the score: phrases are chips in a strip, and selection is made on the piano roll. In the default "Both" view the score gets about 30% of the screen; on a transcription one system is visible.
3. Everything is visible at once, and the app explains itself in prose instead of being self-evident.
4. The project page is a questionnaire ("What do you want to do?") between the player and the music, and sessions are objects to manage.
5. The pencil is a toolbar mode disconnected from bars and phrases; there are no anchored notes, highlights, brackets or fingering.
6. The piano roll is a permanent half of the screen; portrait breaks the panel.
7. The visual identity (dark dashboard, lime accent, [[02-brand-identity]]) reads as software, not as a music stand.

Andrei reviewed the two documents with the team and decided the questions they left open (recorded in the preparation doc): Piece DNA ships after the first redesign release on an extension point built before it; the harmony facts and the missing Try-chip operations are built in the engine before the redesign, not hidden; Pro is a flag with a tester's switch, payment later; the new UI replaces the old one directly, with no flag; the web site is the development environment and the iPad app is production; the iPad app is fully native Swift; the redesign is built in both; marks, bookmarks, resume points and setting overrides travel with the project for Pro, device settings stay on the device, and the free tier has no cloud account; the Strip layout stays as a switchable view with A4 pages as the default; the Teacher category gets a fourth user ink, ochre.

## Decision

### 1. Four layers, two places to work in

The interface has four layers, read from the inside out: **the page** (the only permanent thing), **the marks on it** (kept with the piece), **the overlays** (pulled up from a selection or from the bar and put away with a swipe) and **the library** (outside the piece, visited only to switch or add one). There are two places to be while working on a piece, the page and a focused phrase, and one place to step back to, Piece DNA. Everything else is an overlay that slides over whichever of the two is active and leaves it as it was; no overlay opens another place. A piece opens on its page, at its last resume point, with nothing else on screen.

### 2. The page

- The score is **one continuously scrolling document laid out as A4 pages** ([[0017]] stays the layout unit: A4 proportions, page numbers, the same bar-to-page map as the PDF). Portrait shows one column of pages at about 97% of the width; landscape two pages per row fitted to height. Pinch zooms, a double tap fits. Tapping the top or bottom edge, or the arrow keys, moves by exactly one page; a long swipe up opens the **Pages rail** (thumbnails with phrase numbers and bookmark ticks, and Go to bar). **Strip** (the single system of [[0008]]) remains as a switchable view, a device preference, because a phrase that crosses a page edge reads better on a strip (Andrei); every overlay and mark works on both layouts, from one geometry module (preparation item 11).
- Always visible: the page and, unless hidden, the marks on it. On demand: the **bar** (title, page count, five actions: Phrases, Pen, Piano roll, Insights, more), which fades about two seconds after the last touch and returns on any tap outside the music; the **transport pill** when a phrase is selected or playback runs.
- Direct manipulation: tap a note to play from it; tap a bracket to select its phrase; drag across bars to select freely; long-press a bar for the contextual menu; draw with the stylus, or with a finger while Pen is on.
- Verovio engraves with **Leland** at scale 50 on a 2100 × 2970 page (top margin 500, side margins 150, `spacingSystem` 20), its own header hidden by CSS and the title block set by the app in Source Serif 4; notation follows `--notation` through `currentColor`, so the Slate page works. Bar, system and note boxes come from the rendered `g.measure` and `g.staff` elements, and every overlay is placed from them.

### 3. Phrases on the score

- The score is the selection surface. Detected phrases are **pencil brackets** above the top staff, numbered at their left end; a plum dot at the right end says the phrase has saved ideas. The phrase strip and roll-based selection go away.
- Detection is automatic and hierarchical, **one analysis, three levels** chosen by the player in the Phrases popover of the bar: **Short** (motifs and half-phrases of 1–4 bars), **Phrases** (4–8 bars, the default) and **Sentences** (8–16 or more bars; `SentenceMap`, merged from PR #145). Changing the level redraws and renumbers in place; the selection maps to the enclosing or the contained span. Manual edits (drag a handle, Make phrase, Merge with next, Split here) stick to the piece at their level until Reset to detected; the level is remembered per piece. The 8-bar selection cap goes: a structure the maps know is selected whole (`MotifSelection.MaxStructureBars`).
- **One contextual menu** above whatever is selected: Loop, Focus, Note, Bookmark, then Piano roll, Insights, with the rarer actions (Make phrase, Merge, Split, Highlight, Ask, Reading | As played) in its overflow. It never shows what does not apply. Selection is sticky: opening the roll, the Insights sheet or the Contents drawer, or scrolling away, leaves it in place.

### 4. Phrase focus and the ossia

- Focus is a reading lamp on one phrase, not a different screen: the phrase lifts into a card at the full width, the page stays visible and dimmed behind it, and the transition animates from its place on the page and back. Enter with a second tap on a selected bracket, Focus in the menu or a pinch-out; leave with Back, a tap on the dimmed page or a pinch-in. Previous / next walk the phrases of the chosen level, or, with the Contents toggle, the bookmarks in the player's own order.
- A phrase can hold any number of saved variations. The variation is an **ossia** above the original, bar for bar; a row of chips above it names the variations and picks the one shown, two at once for comparison. **Original / Variation / Alternate** sits in the focus bar only while the phrase has a variation. Hear the transition becomes a **lead-in** toggle (one bar early, one bar late).
- A transcription opens in **Reading** notation; **As played** ([[0016]]) is one tap away in the focus bar and the menu, per phrase and in place, and can be shown as the ossia above the Reading staff. The **Performance** layer shows the performed dynamics, pedal and timing as light marks over the Reading notation.
- Below the lifted phrase a single line, the **Insights strip**, shows the three strongest facts as chips (`FactRanking`, PR #145); a tap opens the stream at that fact.

### 5. Marks kept with the piece

Marks are made on the page, never in a separate screen, and every mark is anchored to a bar or a note id, so it survives zoom, reflow, re-import and the layout switch, travels in the project file and in online saving, and is found again in the Contents drawer's Notes tab.

| Mark | How | On the page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ink** | a stylus always draws, a finger never does; without a stylus, Pen in the bar turns finger drawing on (two fingers scroll); a palette of three inks, the highlighter and the eraser appears on first touch; two-finger tap undoes | vector strokes anchored to the bars under them, 2.2 px round-capped |
| **Margin note** | long-press a bar or a bracket, Note, type or dictate | an amber numbered marker in the margin; a tap opens the note as a popover |
| **Highlight** | select bars, Highlight, a colour; or the palette's highlighter | a translucent amber band behind the staves, multiply blend |
| **Bookmark** | select bars, Bookmark (or B): a title pre-filled with the bar range, category chips, Done | a bracket in the category's ink above the phrase brackets with the title in italic serif; overlapping bookmarks stack |
| **Fingering** | tap a notehead with the stylus or with Pen on, a 1–5 pad; handwritten digits recognised later | small graphite digits above the notehead, keyed by note id |
| **Check flag** | from the import report (scan or transcription), with a reason | an amber "?" square above the uncertain bar, gone when resolved |

- **Bookmarks are the piece's Contents**, written by the player: four categories come with the app, Practise (carmine), Performance (forest), Structure (graphite) and Teacher (ochre), each renamable, with the player's own categories in any of the four inks. The **Contents drawer** (from the title in the bar, the overflow or a swipe from the left edge) has Contents, Notes and Pages tabs, score order or the player's order, drag to reorder, search; a bookmark made from a detected phrase follows it through merges and splits; the detected form (Piece DNA) can be added as Structure bookmarks in one tap. Bookmarks replace the Tag mark of the earlier annotation set ([[0013]] section 10).
- The Pen menu holds the **layers** (Ink, Notes, Bookmarks, Analysis, Check, Performance) and Hide all marks.
- The current pencil store (a separate IndexedDB) is replaced by marks in the project file (preparation items 1 and 2); the palette becomes the four user inks and the amber highlighter of the kit.

### 6. The piano roll as a drawer

The roll slides up from the bottom when asked for (the menu, the bar, the focus bar) and is never a view of its own; "Piano roll only" and "Both" go. It takes about 40% of the height in landscape and 30% in portrait, draggable to 70% (60%), never full screen; the score shifts so the selected phrase stays in view. It shows the selected phrase, or the system under the playhead, bar-aligned with the score at the same horizontal scale; note names on by default, colour by hand (cobalt right, teal left); colour by voice or track and zoom in its own overflow; synchronised both ways; inferred hands keep the dashed outline and "L? / R?" labels. A swipe down puts it away with the selection untouched.

### 7. Insights: one stream from hearing to trying

- The Practise, Understand, Explore and Tools tabs become **one stream in one sheet** (right third in landscape, about 380 px; a half-height bottom sheet in portrait), asked for from a phrase and never present otherwise. Facts for the selected phrase are short claims grouped by Rhythm, Melody, Harmony and Form, narrowed by filter chips; each fact row has the claim, its places (tap to ring them on the score), Evidence folding open in place, a row of **Try chips** and, once something was kept, the idea chips that came from it. Saved ideas gather in a strip at the top and as plum dots on the brackets.
- **Try chips are the experiments, derived from the fact** (preparation item 8: a table from fact kind to catalogue operations, only operations the engine has; a chip whose operation does not exist is not shown). A tap sounds the variation at once as the ossia while the loop keeps playing; **Keep** files it as an idea with the fact that produced it; **Adjust** opens the keep / change / operation controls (today's Build) inline, pre-filled. Surprise me is a shuffle in the sheet header; I have a question is the input at the foot of the stream. Understand, Explore and Build survive as verbs of one stream, not as modes; everything else in [[0013]] (one musical object, one primary selection with context, claim kinds with evidence, the Try it contract, the shared workspace state, the Russian terms) stands.
- The stream re-targets as the selection moves or playback enters a new phrase (a pin holds it); opening the sheet never changes selection, loop, tempo or hands. **Check** is not part of the stream: it is a drawer of its own, since it concerns the import.
- The **AI assistant** is the last voice in the stream, not a chatbot beside the app: asked from a phrase, the focus bar, the bar's overflow (whole piece) or the stream's input, with a scope chip, a folded **What it knows** list of the context it is given (the piece and its facts, the current phrase or bookmark, recent activity, the player's level and settings), and answers that carry **action chips** bound to the app's own primitives (Loop 11.1–11.3, 60%, Right hand, Show on the score, Bookmark as Practise, Add note, Try: Half-time). The conversation is kept with the piece under Notes. It is Pro in full: the free tier shows its entry points and a one-line plum offer, and nothing is sent anywhere. The provider and the widening of [[0003]] (free text and activity history beside the facts digest) are decided in their own record.

### 8. Playback

The transport is a **floating pill** at the bottom centre: Play / Pause, Loop, Tempo (tap for 50 / 75 / 100%, drag for anything else), Metronome, Count-in and Hands (All / Right / Left / Melody / Accompaniment). Position is not a slider: the playhead runs through the score itself. The pill appears with a selection or playback and fades after about three seconds while playing. Loop follows the context: a selection loops the selection, focus loops the phrase, nothing selected loops the page. The score follows playback by scrolling smoothly, keeping the playing system in the upper third; a manual scroll wins until the next Play.

### 9. Library, resume points and import

- The **library** is the one screen outside the piece: the wordmark, one Add piece action, pieces as cards with a real first-page thumbnail, recent work, the saved-ideas tag in plum and the Check tag in amber. One tap opens the piece where it was left. The home page, the project page and the session list collapse into it; nothing asks what the player wants to do.
- **Sessions become resume points** (preparation item 3): opening a piece creates one; it records the page or scroll, the selection or focused phrase, the open drawers, the tempo and the hands, and is updated as the player works. It can be named, several per piece are allowed, Resume opens the latest and a long-press lists them. `SessionIntent` goes; a version 11 file's sessions open as resume points with the task as their name.
- **Import is one flow**: Add piece offers the three routes in one sheet; opening a file runs on the device and is free; scanning a page and transcribing a recording run on the server and are Pro, marked with the plum Pro tag. What leaves the device and the consent stay, as one line and one switch. The report after import is shown once and becomes the **Check layer**: flagged bars with a reason on the score, a Check drawer with Show, Fix notes and Correct, and, for transcriptions, the bar and tempo corrections in its header; the layer disappears when every flag is resolved (preparation items 6 and 12).
- **Account** (sign-in by email code, keep projects online, delete online data, privacy, terms) is a sheet behind the avatar; language and every adjustable element live in Settings.

### 10. Settings: presets, then every switch

- One global Settings sheet (the gear in the library, the overflow in the score; over the page, so changes preview live) with the sections Level, Guidance, Score, Marks and layers, Playback, Piano roll, Assistant, Appearance, Account. Three presets, then a switch for every element: **Learning** (brackets, Insights strip, hints and coach marks, focus suggestions, assistant hints, Check flags all on; Short granularity), **Practising** (the default: brackets and the strip on, hints off, Phrases granularity) and **Clean** (the score, ink and bookmarks only; the pill only while playing; the bar fades in one second; the assistant on request). Changing one switch turns the preset label into Custom. The first run asks once, with the three presets as cards and a one-line description each.
- **Contextual settings**: a long-press on an element (the pill, a bar action, a drawer header, the Insights strip, a bracket) opens a popover with its two or three switches, a This piece / Everywhere scope and a link to the same row in the global sheet. Global settings are the defaults; granularity, layers and a few score settings can be overridden per piece, and the global sheet lists and clears the overrides. Clean page in the overflow is a temporary switch to the Clean preset for this sitting.
- The settings of device features (theme, layout, note names, Pen behaviour, the ink palette) stay on the device; a piece's overrides travel with the project; the free tier has no cloud account at all (Andrei). This replaces the scattered `localStorage` keys with one settings model (preparation item 5).

### 11. Piece DNA

The third place in the app, and the only one that is not the score: a full-screen map of the composition, reached from the library card, the title menu and the overflow, never from the bar's five actions. A form strip across the top (sections on a bar axis with arcs between sections that share material), one lane per defining idea with a mark at every occurrence (filled for exact, hollow for varied, a triangle for inverted), the key lane, then the idea cards (the three to six motifs that make the piece, each with an engraved excerpt, Hear and In the score). Everything plays and points back; Add Structure bookmarks writes the sections into the Contents drawer; the page is defined by its lanes and cards, so new analyses appear as further lanes. Andrei decided that Piece DNA ships **after the first redesign release**, on an extension point built before it (preparation item 18): the data contract in music-core (form sections, motif lanes with the kinds of repeat, the key lane, idea cards; `dna` in the facade, [[0020]]), the route and entry points, and the empty state. The engine work it needs (the form of the whole piece, the main motifs, varied and inverted repeats, key over time) comes afterwards and only fills the contract.

### 12. Pro

The rule for the whole product: **what runs on the device is free; what needs cloud infrastructure or an AI model is Pro.** Facts, Try chips, variations, Piece DNA, bookmarks, ink and playback are free; the assistant, scan and transcription imports and online saving are Pro. The free tier sends nothing anywhere and has no cloud account. For now (preparation item 14) the account carries a Pro flag that the API checks for scanning, transcription, saving online and the assistant, and a **Pro / no-Pro switch** lets testers try both modes (shown only to testers, trusted by the server only for them); payment, through the App Store or the web, comes later.

### 13. The visual system: blue pencil on cream paper

- **Identity.** Cream paper, graphite ink and one blue pencil. The score is black engraving on cream paper; everything the app itself points at (selection, focus, the playhead, the active control, links) is **cobalt**; everything the app has composed (variations, the ossia, saved ideas) is **plum**; everything the player marked (highlighter, note markers, flags) is **amber**; everything the player writes is pencil-coloured ink. The interface around the page is the desk: warm, matte, slightly darker than the paper, so the page is always the brightest surface. Personality: quiet, warm, precise, editorial, unhurried; a music stand with a good lamp. Rules: one accent, never decorative; paper is the brightest surface, no white panels on the page except the contextual menu and dialogs; matte over glossy, soft warm shadows; type does the branding, no gradients or illustration; marks look hand-made (pencil weights, an italic serif).
- **Tokens.** Every colour, type style, space, radius, size, border, shadow, focus ring and motion value is a CSS custom property in `design/kit/tokens.css`, which is the source of truth; components reference tokens only, and no component has a literal colour or size. Two themes with one set of names: **Daylight** on `:root` and **Night stand** on `[data-theme="dark"]`, which keeps the page a dimmed cream so notation stays black on paper, with the **Slate** page (`[data-page="slate"]`, light notation on dark) for a dark room. Contrast is checked against the surface the colour is used on: text 4.5 : 1, large text and graphics 3 : 1 (the table is in the Design system document).
- **User inks** do not change with the theme; they are pigments on the paper: graphite `#3B3733`, carmine `#C8352B`, forest `#2E7D4F` and **ochre `#9C6A0C`** (added by Andrei for the Teacher category, 4.5 : 1 on paper). On the Slate page the inks get a 1 px paper-coloured halo.
- **Type.** Source Serif 4 (500) for the wordmark, titles, piece names and anything that imitates print, including the handwritten style of bookmark titles (italic); Source Sans 3 (400, 500, 600) for every control and body text; tabular numerals everywhere; body never below 15 px on the tablet, nothing interactive below 13 px. Notation is Leland. Both families are OFL and self-hosted as woff2 (`design/kit/fonts`).
- **Icons.** Line icons on a 20 px grid with a 1.5 px stroke, round caps and joins, `currentColor`; 16 px in captions and chips, 20 px in buttons, menus and the pill, 24 px (1.75 px stroke) in the bar; every icon-only control at least 44 × 44 px; an icon never stands alone except play, pause, more, back and close. The named set is `design/kit/icons.js`; Lucide fits for anything generic.
- **Marks on the music** each have one colour and one weight so they never need a legend: the selection band (`--accent-tint` fill, 1.5 px cobalt border, round handles on the first and last barline), the focused phrase (2 px cobalt border, the page under `--dim`), phrase brackets (graphite at 75%, 1.25 px; the selected one cobalt 1.5 px), the playhead (2 px cobalt with a paper halo), fact highlights (a 1.5 px cobalt ring 8 px larger than the notehead), the ossia (1.5 px dashed plum frame), highlighter, margin-note markers, bookmark brackets, fingering, ink and Check flags as in section 5.
- **Components** (`design/kit/ds.css`): the bar (44 px pill, blurred surface, fades), the transport pill (52 px), the contextual menu (44 px pill with a pointer), buttons (40 px; primary cobalt, one per view; secondary, quiet, danger, compact 32 px), the segmented control, chips (28 px) and tags (22 px, tinted by meaning: detected cobalt, assumed hairline grey, check amber, saved plum), the sheet (380 px, one stream, no tabs), drawers, fact rows, cards with real first-page thumbnails, fields, switches, dialogs (420 px), toasts (inverted), the pages rail, the ossia panel, the assistant conversation, the settings sheet (760 px) and popover (260 px), the Piece DNA map. States use three devices everywhere: a tint for hover and selection, a cobalt border or underline for the active thing, opacity (38%) for disabled; hover only on pointer devices; focus rings only for keyboard focus; `prefers-reduced-motion` drops slides to fades.
- **Responsive.** The tokens do not change between devices; page scale, overlay width and hover do. Tablet up to 1180 px, laptop to 1679, desktop from 1680 (pages capped at 1.4 × A4). The bar carries a title, a page count and six icons and fits at 820 px, so it never wraps.
- **Wordmark and icon**: "Motiff Lab" in Source Serif 4 Medium, ink on paper, with the tagline *Hear. Understand. Transform.* in italic at caption size; for the app icon a cream rounded square with one cobalt phrase bracket is proposed and drawn in the visual pass. The brand's name, positioning, voice and messaging ([[02-brand-identity]]) stand; its visual direction is replaced by this section.

### 14. Two apps, one source

- The redesign is built **in both the web app (Blazor) and the native iPad app (SwiftUI)**, each screen web first, checked on the dev site, then in SwiftUI for the iPad release. The iPad app is fully native (Andrei, 2026-10-31); the music-core runs there through NativeAOT behind the facade of [[0020]], and Verovio as a native framework (spike PR #176). Its stack and what stays in the web are decided in **0019**, which supersedes [[0015]]; the Swift shell of 0015 remains until the native app replaces it.
- So that the two do not drift: **one tokens file** (`tokens.json`, generated into `tokens.css` and Swift in the first redesign PR; until then `tokens.css` is the source); **one set of interface strings** (`wwwroot/i18n/*.json`, read by both); **musical text from music-core** (`Words`, [[0012]]); **one SVG icon set**. Both languages ship for the interface and for everything generated (facts and evidence, Try chip names, assistant answers, Piece DNA cards, category names, Check reasons, import warnings), and every control is laid out for the longer Russian labels.

### 15. Rollout

- **The new UI replaces the old one directly, with no flag** (Andrei). Every merge to main deploys the web site, which is the development environment for testing features; the iPad app is production and gets releases, built in Xcode, not single features. Old e2e specs are removed as the screens they test are replaced; new specs are written for the new screens.
- **Order.** The preparation packages first, each mergeable and checkable on its own because the old UI keeps working on the new model: (1) this record, the kit in the repository, the music-core of PR #145 and the issue triage; (2) the piece's data in project file version 12 (marks, bookmarks, resume points, ideas with their fact, the settings model, Check as data, with migration); (3) the engine (phrase levels, the fact-to-chips table, the fact ranking, the score geometry module for A4 and Strip, the ossia prototype, Check for transcriptions, Russian for everything generated, harmony and the missing operations: chord and bass detection #46, Pedal bass V12, More air V7, Ornament, Reharmonise); (4) the Pro flag and the native iPad releases. Then the redesign itself in the order of the Design system: tokens and fonts, Verovio, the bar, the transport pill, the contextual menu, selection and brackets, phrase focus and the ossia, drawers and the sheet, the library, settings.

## Consequences

- Until the preparation packages land, nothing visible changes: the old UI keeps working on the new project file and the new engine results, and each package is checked on the dev site on its own.
- The redesign is presentation only. The UI shows music-core output ([[ui-conventions]]), so every datum a new screen shows exists in the engine first or the screen leaves it out: Harmony facts and the Try chips Reharmonise, Pedal bass, Ornament and More air appear when their operations exist; Piece DNA opens on its empty state until the analysis fills the contract.
- Two UIs share tokens, strings, icons and the engine, so a change to any of them is made once; what differs is layout code (Razor against SwiftUI) and the platform's input (PencilKit, AVAudioEngine, MSAL on the iPad).
- `design/kit` is the reference for every redesign PR and the only place a colour or size is defined; `tokens.css` and the two documents it came with are Andrei's, and a change to the system starts there.
- Open, from the documents: the final wordmark and app icon; the bar's fade timing after use testing; a connected MIDI keyboard as an input (Web MIDI, Core MIDI), which does not affect the redesign; the recognition of handwritten fingering digits, dictated notes and marks in the PDF, which can follow.
