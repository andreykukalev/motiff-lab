# iOS UI and UX

Status: proposal, 2026-10-24. Applies the Stage 1 workspace as it is built today (`src/MotiffLab.Web`, [../ui-conventions.md](../ui-conventions.md)) to iPad and iPhone. Where touch needs a different gesture, the action and its result stay the same as on the web: music-core decides what a selection or an edit is.

## Principles for touch

1. **Fingers are not a mouse.** No hover, no right click, no Shift. Anything the web shows as a `title` tooltip (why a button is disabled, what a Keep chip's state means, the basis of a fact) must be visible text, or one tap away.
2. **One finger moves the view; deliberate gestures select.** Panning is the most common gesture on a small screen, so it gets the plainest one. Selection uses the bar ruler, a long press, or the Pencil.
3. **Targets of 44 points or more.** Notes at normal zoom are smaller than that, so a tap picks the nearest note within reach (see below), and the edit controls live in panels with full-size buttons.
4. **Play is always one thumb away.** The transport stays on screen in every layout and every sheet height.
5. **The music stand layout comes first.** iPad landscape is the main layout; everything else is derived from it, as the MVP UX spec (04) says.
6. **Same look as the web.** Brand tokens from `docs/02-brand-identity.md` (dark workspace, Electric Lime as the one accent), the same certainty language, the same labels. System controls (sheets, menus, share sheet, document picker) are iOS-native so the app feels at home.

## Screens

Stage 1 on iOS has the same screens as the web, in the same order.

| Web | iOS |
| --- | --- |
| Projects (`Home.razor`, N1) | Projects list. iPad: a sidebar of projects with the open project beside it. iPhone: a full-screen list. Import is the primary button; the starter library row stays |
| Import (`Import.razor`, N2) | The system document picker (Files, iCloud Drive, other providers), plus "Open in Motiff Lab" from Files, Mail and AirDrop, and drag and drop on iPad. The import report is the same screen |
| Playback lab and workspace (`PlaybackLab.razor`, N3 to N5) | The workspace below |
| Settings (AI on or off, "What is sent") | A Settings screen reached from the projects list, plus the licence and credits page (piano samples, Verovio) |

## The workspace

The web workspace has: a transport row, a toolbar (Notes filter, Hear, score switch), the roll and the score side by side, and below them the selection panel (loop, clear, V1 reshape, steps, Hear original and variation, export, Keep presets and chips, Edit notes by hand, Correct notes) next to the facts panel. On iOS the same parts are arranged by size class.

### iPad, landscape (main layout)

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ‹ Projects   Für Elise · bars 1–2      [Notes ▾] [Hear ▾] [Score ▾] ⓘ │
├───────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ bar ruler  1 · 2 · 3 · 4              │                               │
│ piano roll (one finger pans,          │   Inspector                   │
│ pinch zooms, ruler selects)           │   [Selection] [Facts] [Edit]  │
│                                       │                               │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤   Loop · Clear · bars 1–2     │
│ score (as written / generated)        │   Keep presets, Keep chips    │
│                                       │   Reshape parameters          │
├───────────────────────────────────────┤   Accept · Reject · ↶ · ↷     │
│ keyboard (multi-touch)                │   Explanation and flags       │
├───────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┤
│ ▶  ⟲ loop   ◀ position ▶   tempo 100%   metronome   count-in   A | B   │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- The canvas takes the left two thirds, the inspector the right third, as in the MVP UX spec. Inside the canvas, the roll and the score stack vertically instead of side by side; a split handle moves the boundary, and the score can be hidden to give the roll the full height.
- The inspector holds what sits below the workspace on the web, in three tabs: **Selection** (loop, clear, Keep, reshape, steps, Hear, export), **Facts** (the `FactsPanel` sections), **Edit** (Edit notes by hand, Correct notes). The inspector carries the same `data-keeps-selection` rule: nothing inside it clears the selection.
- The transport sits at the bottom, where a thumb rests on a tablet on the stand.
- The inspector can collapse to a narrow strip to give the canvas the full width; with Stage Manager or Split View making the window narrow, the layout falls back to the iPad portrait or iPhone layout by size class, not by device.

### iPad, portrait

- Roll above score, full width; the inspector becomes a bottom sheet with three heights: **peek** (selection summary, Loop and Clear), **medium** (Keep, reshape, steps), **large** (everything, including Facts and Edit tabs).
- The sheet never covers the transport.

### iPhone, portrait

- One canvas at a time: a segmented control **Roll | Score** above it (the web's "Both" layout does not fit). The keyboard strip under the roll can be hidden.
- The toolbar (Notes, Hear, Score) collapses into one menu button in the top bar.
- The transport is two rows: play, loop and position on the first; tempo, metronome and count-in on the second, with secondary controls in a sheet (as 04 already specifies for phones).
- The inspector is a bottom sheet with the same three heights. Peek shows the selection's bars and the Loop button; dragging it up reveals Keep and the reshape parameters.
- The 8-bar selection limit is the same; on a phone only about two bars fit at once, so selecting more than two bars uses the ruler with auto-scroll at the edges.

### iPhone, landscape ("on the stand")

- The roll and keyboard fill the screen with the transport as a translucent strip; the inspector opens as a side sheet from the right.
- The screen does not lock while the piece plays or loops.

## Gesture map

This replaces the mouse rules in [../ui-conventions.md](../ui-conventions.md) "Selection on the piano roll" for touch. The results are the same: the roll reports where a gesture started and ended, and music-core snaps it (`MotifSelector.Drag`).

| Action | Web (mouse and keyboard) | iOS touch | iPad with Pencil, trackpad or keyboard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scroll the roll in time and pitch | Wheel, scrollbars | One-finger drag in the note area; flick to coast | Two-finger trackpad scroll |
| Zoom | Plus and minus keys | Pinch: horizontal pinch zooms time, vertical pinch zooms pitch | Pinch on the trackpad; plus and minus on the keyboard |
| Select a range | Drag anywhere | Drag along the **bar ruler**; or **long press** (about 0.3 s, with a light haptic) in the note area, then drag | Pencil drag anywhere selects, the finger still pans. Trackpad click-drag behaves like the mouse |
| Select one bar | Click a bar number | Tap a bar number | Same |
| Extend to a bar | Shift-click a bar number | Drag the nearer edge grip to it | Shift-tap with a hardware keyboard |
| Move one edge | Drag an edge grip | Drag an edge grip. Grips are 44-point handles on the ruler and stay visible while a selection exists | Same |
| Hear and select a note | Click a note | Tap a note | Same |
| Add a note to the selection | Shift, Ctrl or Cmd click | Turn on **Add notes** (a toggle in the selection summary), then tap notes to add or remove; or two-finger tap a note | Shift-tap or Cmd-tap |
| Clear the selection | Escape, click an empty place, click outside the roll | Tap an empty place in the roll, or **Clear** in the selection summary. Taps in the inspector, sheets and score never clear | Escape on a hardware keyboard |
| Play or pause | Space | The play button in the transport, which is on screen in every layout | Space on a hardware keyboard |
| Hear a key | Click or drag across keys | Touch keys; **each finger is its own voice**, so chords play; slide to glide across keys | Same |
| Seek | Position slider, click a note in the score | Drag the position scrubber; tap a note in the score | Same |
| Undo and redo a step | Buttons | Buttons; the system three-finger swipe left and right | Cmd-Z and Shift-Cmd-Z |

### Tapping small notes

- A tap selects the note whose rectangle is under the finger; if none is, the nearest note onset within 22 points of the touch, preferring the note sounding at that tick.
- If two or more notes are equally near (a chord), the first tap selects the top note and shows a small chooser above the finger listing the chord's notes by name (E5, C5, A4); a tap in the chooser picks one. This avoids guessing on a dense chord.
- A selected note gets a lime outline thick enough to see under a fingertip and a label with its pitch name and position.

### Haptics

Light haptic ticks when a dragged edge snaps to a beat, a stronger one at a bar line, and one when the 8-bar limit is reached (with the message from music-core). Haptics are off when the system setting is off.

## Panels on touch

### Hints without hover

On the web many explanations live in `title` attributes: the reason Accept is disabled (`AcceptDisabledReason`), each Keep chip's status text, preset hints, the basis of a fact, what each parameter button does. On iOS:

- **Disabled controls show their reason as text** under the control row, not only on hover. One line, the text music-core returns.
- **Keep chips** show their state by shape and icon as well as colour: kept (lime, filled, check), not kept (outline), broken by this step (dashed, warning icon). A tap toggles; a long press shows the status text in a callout.
- **Facts** keep the certainty label as a visible badge (detected, inferred, uncertain) on every fact, and the basis opens with a tap on the fact (a disclosure row), never only on hover.
- An **ⓘ button** in the top bar turns on "explain controls" mode: tapping any control shows its hint instead of acting. Useful in the study and for first use.

### Reshape and variation controls

- Parameter buttons are one-tap chips that apply and play at once (S1-E3), laid out in a horizontal scroll row on the iPhone and a wrapping grid in the iPad inspector.
- **Accept step** is the primary button; **Reject step**, **Undo** and **Redo** sit in the same row, as on the web. The breadcrumbs ("Original › Half-time › trying Later by 1 eighth") sit above the row and scroll horizontally when long.
- The explanation and the rule flags (`Variation.Flags`) are always listed, never truncated without a "More" control.
- **Hear original | Hear variation | Both** is a segmented control mirrored in the transport as A | B, so A/B switching at the same position is one tap without opening the inspector.
- **Export motif MIDI** and **Export variation MIDI** open the system share sheet with the file `MidiExporter` wrote, named by `MidiExporter.FileName`. "Save to Files" is in that sheet; nothing is sent anywhere the player did not pick.

### Editing notes by hand and correcting imported notes

The web tables (one row per note with nudge buttons for pitch, onset, length and velocity, plus Add after, Remove and Revert) do not fit a phone. On iOS:

- **Select, then nudge.** Selecting a note on the roll or in the list opens an **edit pad** in the inspector or sheet: four rows (Pitch ▼▲, Start ◀▶, Length −+, Velocity −+), each with full-size buttons, and Add after, Remove and Revert below. The steps are the same as the web: a semitone, a 16th, 10 of velocity. Every press is one call to `VariationChain.Edit` or one `NoteEditLog` entry, as now.
- **The list stays available.** The Edit tab lists the notes as heard (or the selected notes) as compact rows; tapping a row selects that note on the roll and opens the edit pad.
- **Direct drag is an explicit edit mode, not the default.** An **Edit on roll** toggle in the Edit tab makes a one-finger drag on a selected note move it by whole semitones and 16ths, with a ghost of where it will land. On lift the UI turns the drag into the same nudges (for example two semitones up and one 16th later) and sends them as one edit, so music-core still makes and checks the change and Undo takes it back in one step. Outside that mode a drag on a note pans or, after a long press, selects. This keeps editing deliberate and stops accidental moves while playing.
- Edited notes keep the lime edge and the "Edited by you" label; removed notes are listed with a Revert button each, as on the web.
- After the first correction the score switches to the score generated from the notes, as on the web, and says so.

### Facts

- One collapsible section per `FactLens` in enum order, each header showing the count and how many are detected, inferred or uncertain, as on the web. Sections that the player closed stay closed as the selection changes.
- In the iPad inspector, Facts is its own tab so the facts and the Keep chips can be compared by switching tabs without losing the selection; on the iPhone it is a tab in the large sheet.

### Score

- Tap a note to seek to its onset; the playing notes are marked and the view scrolls with them, as now.
- Pinch zooms the score; Verovio lays out again at the new scale after the pinch ends, not during it.
- In iPad portrait and iPhone the score can go full screen as a "music stand" view with only the transport, for reading while playing.

## Projects, import and export

- The projects list shows the same summary as the web (file name, motifs, variations, last opened). Swipe actions: **Export** (share sheet with the `.motiff.json` file) and **Delete** (with confirmation; there is no cloud copy).
- Importing a project file (`.motiff.json`) from the web or another device works the same as importing a MIDI or MusicXML file.
- A storage line in Settings shows how much space projects use, as the web projects screen does.

## Accessibility

- **VoiceOver.** The canvas roll is a single element for VoiceOver with a clear label (as on the web), plus custom rotor actions: next and previous note, next and previous bar, select this bar, extend selection, play from here. The Edit tab's list is the full accessible alternative to the roll: every note can be selected, heard and edited from it.
- **Dynamic Type** for all panel text; the canvases keep their own scale and are zoomed with pinch.
- **Not by colour alone.** Certainty, hands, kept or broken Keep chips and variation differences differ by shape, pattern or icon, as the brand rules require.
- **Reduce Motion** stops the auto-scroll animation of the roll and score; they jump instead.
- **Left-handed use.** The iPad inspector can sit on the left (a setting), so the hand not playing reaches it.

## Hardware keyboard, trackpad and Pencil on iPad

- Keyboard shortcuts match the web: Space plays and pauses, Escape clears, arrow keys move the note selection, plus and minus zoom; Cmd-Z and Shift-Cmd-Z undo and redo steps; the system shortcut overlay (holding Cmd) lists them.
- A trackpad or mouse gets the web behaviour: hover, click-drag selects, Shift-click extends.
- The Pencil selects anywhere on the roll while fingers pan and zoom; Pencil hover (on models that have it) shows the note or bar under the tip. Pencil annotation on the score is a Stage 4 idea, not Stage 1.

## States to design

All the web states (unsupported or damaged file, no usable notes, very large file, AI off, offline, rate-limited or fallback) plus:

- **Audio interrupted** by a call, Siri or another app: playback pauses at the current tick and resumes from there on Play.
- **Headphones unplugged or Bluetooth disconnected:** playback pauses, as iOS apps are expected to.
- **Bluetooth output** with high latency: a one-line note that the playhead is compensated but playing along will feel late; wired or USB is better for Stage 2.
- **Silent switch on:** playback still sounds (the audio session is set for playback, as the web already requests where Safari supports it).
- **Low storage on the device:** suggest exporting and deleting old projects.
- **Opened from another app with an unsupported file:** the same plain reason and tip as the web import.
