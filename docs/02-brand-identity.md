# Motiff Lab · Brand Identity

## Name

The product is called **Motiff Lab**: a place to experiment with motifs, the small musical ideas that can be repeated, developed and transformed. It replaces the working title "Motif Lab" used in earlier tabs, which now refers to the same product.

- **Motiff:** a musical motif, the smallest meaningful unit of a musical idea.
- **Lab:** a laboratory for experiment and discovery, where trying things matters more than getting them perfect the first time.
- Together: music is not only something to reproduce; it is something to explore, understand and make your own.

**Writing the name**

| Rule | Correct | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two words, both capitalised | Motiff Lab | MotiffLab, motiff lab, MOTIFF LAB in running text |
| Double f, always | Motiff | Motif Lab (the old working title) |
| Short form, after the first mention in a text | Motiff | ML, Lab |
| Pronunciation | "mo-TEEF lab", like the musical word | "MO-tiff" |

**Why the double f, and what it risks**

- It makes the name distinctive and more likely to be ownable: "motif" is a common word, and the music world already has well-known products called Motif, for example Yamaha's MOTIF synthesizer line.
- It may be read as a typo or auto-corrected to "Motif". The logo, app-store listing and first-run copy should show the spelling clearly, and the spelling test in the verification list checks how people type it after hearing it.

## Positioning, promise and tagline

Motiff Lab stands for a way of engaging with music, not for a particular AI technology or input format; that is what lets the brand cover notation in Stage 1 and audio in Stage 3.

**Category:** musical exploration and improvisation workspace.

**Positioning statement:** For self-directed musicians who want to understand and play the music they love, Motiff Lab is an AI-assisted musical exploration workspace that turns any motif into an experiment. Unlike transcription tools or generic AI music generators, it builds the musician's own understanding and creativity instead of handing them answers.

**Core promise:** Turn the music you love into music of your own.

**Tagline:** Hear. Understand. Transform.

| Word | What it means | Where it lives in the product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hear | Discover musical ideas by listening and exploring | Listen, loop and compare; playback of every change |
| Understand | Reveal how melody, rhythm, harmony and structure relate | Explore lenses, explanations, the Understand mode |
| Transform | Make variations, try new possibilities, develop your own voice | Transform mode, the variation engine, Perform |

**Campaign line:** Every motif is a possibility.

**Philosophy:** The goal isn't just to reproduce music. It's to understand it well enough to transform it.

**Brand principle:** Music is not only something to learn. It is something to understand, explore and transform.

These lines echo the product philosophy in the Overview: move from information to understanding, reconstruction, transformation and, finally, independence.

## Personality and voice

Motiff Lab balances the precision of a technical tool with the freedom of a creative studio. It talks like a knowledgeable musician friend: curious, exact, encouraging, and honest about what it does not know.

| Trait | What it means | Sounds like | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curious | Invites exploration and asks musical questions | "What happens if the bass stays and the top moves?" | Telling the player what to feel |
| Intelligent | Explains relationships clearly and accurately | "The E arrives before the chord change and belongs to the next chord." | Jargon without explanation; vague praise |
| Creative | Makes experiment easy and rewarding | "Try it with a broken-chord left hand." | Promising a hit or a "better" version |
| Precise | Gives real control and exact facts | "2 notes now start an 8th early." | Rounding off what changed |
| Approachable | Welcomes players without much theory | "Contour: the shape of the melody, up and down." | Condescension; textbook tone |
| Independent | Builds the player's own skill, not reliance on AI | "Try your own version first. Ideas unlock after." | Presenting AI output as the answer |

**Voice rules**

1. Plain words first; a music term is explained the first time it appears on a screen.
2. Name the change, then the effect, never the other way round.
3. The AI suggests; the player decides. Generated material is "an idea", never "the answer".
4. Say when the app is unsure, and how sure it is.
5. Never shame the use of help: hints and full reveals are tools, not failures.
6. Celebrate exploration and progress, not perfection or speed.

**UI copy examples**

| Moment | Write | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variation explanation | "The melody keeps its notes; the left hand now plays an Alberti pattern in 8ths." | "Your variation is great!" |
| Discovery lock | "Make your own version first. Ideas unlock after you save it." | "Locked." |
| Uncertain chord | "Am or C/A? Both fit these notes." | "Chord: Am" shown as fact |
| Disabled operation | "Needs chords. This motif is a single melody line." | "Unavailable." |
| Import error | "This looks like a PDF. Motiff Lab reads MIDI and MusicXML; most notation apps can export MusicXML." | "Error: unsupported format." |
| Full reveal | "Show all notes? You can always hide them again." | "Give up and see the answer?" |

## Visual direction

<!-- Replaced on 2026-11-01 by the redesign (decision record 0018, section 13): the earlier direction, a dark workspace with an Electric Lime accent, is in the git history and in docs/source/04-brand-identity-motiff-lab.md. -->

**Blue pencil on cream paper.** Editors and conductors have always marked printed music with a blue pencil: it is visible on the page, it never competes with the engraving, and it washes out when the music is photographed. Motiff Lab borrows that convention. The score is black engraving on cream paper; everything the app itself points at is cobalt; everything the musician writes is pencil-coloured ink; everything the app has composed (a variation, an idea) is plum; everything the musician marked (highlighter, notes, flags) is amber. The interface around the page is the desk: warm, matte, slightly darker than the paper, so the page is always the brightest thing on screen.

Personality in five words: quiet, warm, precise, editorial, unhurried. The identity reads as a music stand with a good lamp, not as software.

**Rules that follow from it**

- One accent. Cobalt is the only colour that means "interactive" or "selected". It is never used decoratively.
- Paper is the brightest surface. No white panels sit on top of the page except the small contextual menu and dialogs.
- Matte over glossy. Sheets and pills are translucent surfaces with a blur, not glass; shadows are soft and warm, never black.
- Type does the branding. A serif for titles and piece names, as on a printed edition; a humanist sans for controls; no gradients, no illustration.
- Marks look hand-made. Phrase brackets, bookmarks and fingering use pencil weights and an italic serif, so the musician's own marks and the app's analysis sit on the page the way pencil does.

**Core palette** (`design/kit/tokens.css` is the source of truth; contrast is against the surface the colour is normally used on, WCAG 2.x)

**User inks** do not change with the theme: they are pigments on the paper, which is cream in both themes. They are also the four default bookmark categories: graphite `#3B3733` (Structure), carmine `#C8352B` (Practise), forest `#2E7D4F` (Performance) and ochre `#9C6A0C` (Teacher, 4.5 : 1 on paper). On the Slate page the inks get a 1 px paper-coloured halo.

**Typography**

- Source Serif 4 (500) for the wordmark, titles, piece names and anything that imitates print; bookmark titles and ink labels in its italic, as handwriting. Source Sans 3 (400, 500, 600) for every control and body text. The two families are drawn by the same designer, so their x-heights and weights match. Numerals are tabular everywhere, so bar numbers, tempo and times do not jitter.
- Notation is Leland (the MuseScore SMuFL font, OFL) through Verovio.
- Both families are OFL, available on Google Fonts and self-hosted as woff2 (`design/kit/fonts`); they cover Lithuanian, Russian and the other European scripts the app ships in.
- Readability: body never below 15 px on the tablet; nothing interactive below 13 px; line length in sheets 45 to 65 characters; captions in `--ink-2`, never `--ink-3`, when they carry information.

**Graphic elements**

- Icons are line icons on a 20 px grid with a 1.5 px stroke, round caps and joins, in `currentColor` (`design/kit/icons.js`); the music-specific ones (phrases, piano roll, metronome, lead-in, hands, tempo dial) are drawn on the same grid.
- The phrase bracket (the ⎡ ⎤ shape drawn above the staff) is the brand's one graphic motif and the proposed app icon: a cream rounded square with a single cobalt bracket.
- The wordmark is "Motiff Lab" set in Source Serif 4 Medium, ink on paper, with the tagline *Hear. Understand. Transform.* in the same serif, italic, at caption size. No symbol is needed inside the app.
- Motion is quiet: 120 ms for hover, press and toggle, 200 ms for menus and chips, 320 ms for sheets and the phrase lifting into focus, one easing curve; `prefers-reduced-motion` drops slides to fades.

## The brand in the product

The brand is the page itself: cream paper, black engraving, and one cobalt pencil that always points to what the player chose. The mapping below is the Design system's; the screens that show it are in `design/kit/screens`.

| Product element | Brand treatment |
| --- | --- |
| The score | an A4 sheet on the desk, engraved in Leland, with a soft paper shadow; the page is always the brightest surface |
| What the app points at: selection, focus, playhead, active control | cobalt, with its tint for bands and active backgrounds |
| What the app composed: the ossia, variations, saved ideas | plum, dashed frame for the ossia, a dot on the bracket for a saved idea |
| What the player marked: highlighter, margin notes, Check flags | amber |
| What the player wrote: ink, bookmarks, fingering | the four user inks, pencil weights, an italic serif for titles |
| Claim kinds on facts | tags tinted by meaning: detected cobalt, assumed hairline grey, check amber, saved plum |
| Themes | Daylight; Night stand keeps the page cream; the Slate page for a dark room |
| Explanations and facts | Source Sans 3, sentence case, in the brand voice, in the player's language |
| First run and empty states | messaging library lines; the tagline appears once, on the first screen |
| Sound | a neutral, warm sampled piano; no interface sound effects |

**Feature names follow the tagline:** Hear, Understand and Transform are what the player does from a phrase, as the verbs of one Insights stream (Try, Keep, Adjust); "Keep" is the name for preserving choices.

## Verification and open decisions

The identity is not final until the name is cleared; these checks should finish before anything public (website, store listing, social accounts) goes live.

**Before going public**

- [ ] Trademark search for "Motiff Lab" and "Motiff" in the EU (EUIPO), US (USPTO) and international (WIPO) registers, in software, education and online-service classes.
- [ ] Check for confusion with existing music marks named Motif, such as Yamaha's MOTIF synthesizers.
- [ ] Domain availability: motifflab.com, .app and .io, plus the Lithuanian .lt if useful.
- [ ] App Store and Google Play: no existing app with a confusingly similar name.
- [ ] Social handles on the main platforms.
- [ ] Spelling test: 10 people hear the name and type it; at least 7 should spell it right.
- [ ] Pronunciation test with the same people.
- [ ] Confirm typeface licences for web and app.

**Open decisions**

- [x] Final accent: cobalt, the blue pencil (decision record 0018, 2026-10-31).
- [x] Themes: Daylight is the default; Night stand and the Slate page are equal from the redesign.
- [ ] Wordmark and app icon: drawn in the visual pass from the direction above.
- [x] The palette is design tokens shared by the web app and the iPad app (`design/kit/tokens.css`).

Once the name is cleared, "Motif Lab" in earlier tabs can be replaced with "Motiff Lab".
